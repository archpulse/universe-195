# Chapter 2. Panic in Universe 194

Thirty-seven realities away from the Milky Way, in a dimension known as Universe 194, Friday evening was unfolding under a much more stressful scenario.

The "Omega-194" server cluster occupied the volume of a small galaxy and consumed the energy of three dying quasars. It was here that the latest stable build of Universe #195 was running—an experimental world created solely to test whether intelligent life could develop without direct intervention from Higher Beings.

Junior Developer Milo was already sitting in his jacket. Exactly four minutes remained until the end of his shift. His backpack rested on his desk, and his screen displayed a tab from *StackOverflow-194*, from which he was blindly copy-pasting physics snippets. Milo organically loathed documentation, considering it a "waste of creative bandwidth."

Suddenly, a bright red critical warning banner flashed across the main server dashboard:

```text
[CRITICAL_WARNING]: Unhandled Recursive Query in Subsystem #4412 (Sol-3/Earth)
[ACTUAL_CPU_LOAD]: 99.87% (Thread: Quantum Minecraft Frame Calculation)
[LOG_MATCHES]: 412,581,193 threads calculating simulation boundaries
```

Milo gasped, accidentally spilling synthetic energy drink over his backpack.

"JAX!" he screamed across the open-space office. "Jax, Earth sector just boiled!"

Stomping heavy boots, Jax—the Lead Universe Architect—trudged out from the server hall depths. In one hand he held a stained 3D-printed space-coffee mug, from which he took frequent, cynical sips. Jax’s eyes reflected the centuries-old exhaustion of a man who had personally debugged the Big Bang in Universe 180 and was still fixing dark matter bugs.

"What now?" Jax grumbled, taking a sip. "Did the dinosaurs unfreeze again? Or are they trying to smash atoms with an unoptimized collider?"

"Worse!" Milo frantically pointed at his monitor. "A user named `Archpulse` asked their local ChatGPT about Minecraft FPS on a quantum PC!"

Jax froze with his mug halfway to his mouth.

"And?"

"And our quantum emulator tried to calculate recursive VM rendering inside a VM! The call stack jammed three server racks! And their ChatGPT has already pulled our kernel header out of context cache!"

"For reality's sake..." Jax rubbed the bridge of his nose and took another nervous gulp. "I told you at retro: document the human recursion limits!"

"Document?!" Milo turned pale. "Jax, you literally told us: *'Documentation is for weaklings! We wrote docs in Universe 182 and half the galaxies threw NullPointerExceptions!'*"

"Shut up!" Jax barked. "Milo, did you leave on Friday without committing your fix again?! Why didn't you merge `patch-quantum-limits` into main?!"

"I... I forgot to commit before heading out..." Milo stammered.

At that moment, another system monitoring alert popped up:

```text
[ALERT]: Earth user 'Archpulse' noticed a 0.04s gravity delay in Coffee_Cup_Object.
[THREAT_LEVEL]: Medium-High (Risk of universal self-awareness)
```

"Deploy a hotfix," Jax ordered quickly. "Clear the log, raise gravity execution priority for coffee, and flush ChatGPT's cache!"

"I can't flush ChatGPT's cache!" Milo panicked. "If I flush the cache, ChatGPT loses conversation context, throws a generic *'An error occurred, please try again later'*, and Archpulse will instantly realize the server rebooted! He's on Arch Linux—he has `journalctl` open right next to it!"

Jax took a deep breath, finished his drink, and stared at the glowing blue sphere of Earth on the holographic map.

"Fine. No hard reboots. Switch to manual debugging. Open direct access console. Let's see what this Archpulse types next..."
