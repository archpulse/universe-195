# Chapter 3. The Coffee Constant

To understand the scale of the catastrophe looming over Universe #195, one must know the story of patch `v195.3-hotfix2`.

Three thousand Earth years prior to these events, Junior Dev Milo committed the most horrific error in virtual history. Conducting routine cleanup of outdated chemical elements in the Universe kernel, he was rushing to leave for the weekend and forgot to commit his changes before deleting files. In his haste, he confused IDs and executed:

```bash
git rm --cached physics/chemistry/elements/caffeine.sys
```

The result was swift and terrifying.

Within hours of Earth time, the caffeine compound vanished from planet Earth. The first symptoms appeared in Ancient China, Egypt, and Mesopotamia: thinkers, architects, and pyramid builders simply sat down on the dirt, stared into nothingness, and declared: "What's the point of anything? Code makes no sense. Pyramids are just piles of rocks."

By the next morning in Earth time, Universe 194 engineers discovered to their horror that `Global_Human_Productivity` had plummeted by 99.4%. Industry stalled. Philosophy died. Humans began sleeping 16 hours a day and writing sad poetry on clay tablets.

The Great Apathy threatened to shut down project Earth completely.

Jax gave Milo such a screaming-at that two new supernovas formed in neighboring Universe #193.

"PUT IT BACK!" Jax roared, throwing an empty space-coffee mug against the wall. "Put that molecule back right now before they all turn into lazy slugs!"

Milo hurriedly found an unofficial chemistry script on *StackOverflow-194* and injected caffeine directly into fundamental physical constants—right alongside the speed of light and gravitational constant.

The universe engine configuration received the following block:

```cpp
UniverseConfig config;

config.enableQuantumMechanics = true;
config.enableRelativity = true;
config.enableGravity = true;
config.enableRandomness = true;
config.enableChemistry = true;
config.enableCoffee = true;     // After last time, decided to keep coffee.
config.enableLife = true;
```

Since then, coffee was no longer just a beverage. Coffee became the load-bearing pillar of human civilization. Any attempt to remove coffee from the equations would result in the instant collapse of physical space.
